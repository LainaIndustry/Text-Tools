How Many Words Should a Blog Post Have?

BloggingJanuary 22, 20266 min read

Introduction

There is no universal answer to how long a blog post should be. The right length depends on the topic, the audience, and the goal of the piece. A quick announcement might be 300 words, while a comprehensive guide might run to 3,000 words or more. This article explains how to decide what works for your situation.

Common Word Count Ranges

Based on common practice in online publishing, these ranges are useful starting points:

  • Short posts (300–600 words): news updates, announcements, quick tips.
  • Standard posts (800–1,200 words): most how-to articles and opinion pieces.
  • Long-form posts (1,500–2,500 words): in-depth guides, tutorials, comparison posts.
  • Pillar content (3,000+ words): comprehensive resources meant to rank for broad topics.

These are guidelines, not rules. A well-written 700-word post can outperform a padded 2,000-word post every time.

Match the Length to the Audience

Consider who is reading and what they need. A busy professional may prefer a concise summary, while someone researching a complex topic may want depth. Ask what question the reader came to answer, then give them exactly what they need to answer it — no more, no less.

Match the Length to the Topic

Some topics require more explanation. A recipe might need 500 words; a guide to choosing a database might need 3,000. If you find yourself padding to reach a word count, the topic probably does not need that many words.

What About SEO?

Word count is one of many factors that can influence search rankings, but it is not a direct ranking requirement. Search engines aim to surface content that best answers a query. If your post answers the query well at 800 words, there is no need to stretch it to 2,000.

For a deeper look, see our article on word count for SEO.

Conclusion

Write as many words as the topic requires and no more. Use a word counter to check your count as you go, and focus on clarity and usefulness rather than hitting an arbitrary number.
